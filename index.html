ICAIF 2026 Workshop on AI-Driven Market Microstructure Milan, November 2026

Where Does Learned In-Context Policy Mirror Descent Pay Off in Market Making? A Component Diagnosis

Xinyue Wu, Yuhe Sui, Shufang Chen, Yingzhi Tang

Returns hide which learned part of a market maker fails after a regime shift. We take a frozen Transformer that runs policy mirror descent in context, with a learned actor and a learned critic, and place it in a market small enough to solve exactly. Under fee, volatility, liquidity and toxicity stress we swap each learned part for its exact counterpart and measure value capture, not P&L. Against the best fixed quoting rule, the learned loop wins under severe fee and toxicity stress and loses on nominal, volatile and illiquid markets.

Actor
Stays within margin on all nine market families.
Critic
Gives up value on every one of them.
So
Improve the critic stage first.

PolicyAttention: Robustness and Failure Modes of Softmax Policy Improvement

Yuhe Sui, Yingzhi Tang, Xinyue Wu

A softmax policy update can be exact on paper and brittle once implemented, because finite attention leaks across groups of states. We give a certificate that bounds how far the returned policy can be from optimal given actor and critic error, and an exact boundary beyond which a stronger update makes the policy worse.

ICAIF 2026IAFM workshop

PolicyAttention: A Certifiable and Testable Update Rule for Attention-Based Allocation Controllers

Shufang Chen, Yuhe Sui, Yingzhi Tang, Xinyue Wu

An allocation controller can look like policy mirror descent without obeying it. An exact identity turns the claim into something testable: it predicts two response slopes and no reaction when every action value shifts by the same amount. We test five trained Transformers in a synthetic allocation problem with four regimes and three exposures.

Slopes
All five match both, with median errors under 0.5%.
Shift invariance
All five fail it, by a small but systematic amount.
